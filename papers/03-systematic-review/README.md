## Abstract

Non-communicable diseases are the leading and most rapidly accelerating cause of preventable mortality across sub-Saharan Africa — yet the clinical AI literature has overwhelmingly neglected both the African epidemiological context and the algorithmic fairness implications of deploying ML models in these settings.

This paper presents the **first systematic review and empirical analysis** of algorithmic fairness in clinical ML for NCD prediction oriented toward sub-Saharan Africa. Following a PRISMA-inspired protocol, **47 studies published between 2015 and 2026** were identified, screened, and analyzed.

## Five-Pillar Research Agenda

| Pillar | Focus |
|:---|:---|
| **1. Data Representation** | Addressing the African patient data crisis |
| **2. Fairness Measurement** | Closing the gap in formal fairness metric adoption |
| **3. Explainability** | Mandating interpretability in clinical AI for low-resource settings |
| **4. Deployment Infrastructure** | Removing barriers to real-world implementation |
| **5. Sex & Demographic Bias** | Treating demographic fairness as a clinical safety issue |
